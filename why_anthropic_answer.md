# Why Anthropic?

I spent the last year in healthcare helping ophthalmologists decide whether to trust systems that could one day diagnose their patients' blindness. The conversations were never about features or performance benchmarks. They were about: *What happens when this gets it wrong? Who's accountable? How do I explain this to my patient?*

That's when I realized the companies that will matter in the next decade aren't the ones racing to deploy first—they're the ones building systems people can actually trust in high-stakes environments. That's Anthropic.

**Here's what I understand about partner marketing that most people miss:** It's not about convincing skeptics through better slides. It's about finding organizations that already share your values but lack the language, frameworks, or confidence to act on them. My job at Topcon wasn't to "sell" healthcare systems on innovation—it was to help them articulate why it mattered to their patients and give them the tools to move forward with integrity. That's how I drove 200% growth in engagement: I found the people who cared, and I made it easier for them to act.

The partners Anthropic needs aren't the ones chasing hype. They're enterprise leaders, researchers, and operators who understand that cutting corners now creates catastrophic risk later. They're the CMO who wants to transform customer service but won't sacrifice user privacy. The product team that needs to scale globally but refuses to compromise on quality. The healthcare executive who sees the potential but can't afford to be wrong.

**These are my people.** I've coordinated cross-functional initiatives across five teams and two continents. I've worked with key opinion leaders, startups, and health systems to co-create programs that balance innovation with responsibility. I speak seven languages—not as a party trick, but because I've learned that meeting people in their context, their culture, and their concerns is the only way to build real partnerships.

What excites me about Anthropic isn't just that you're solving hard technical problems—it's that you're solving the *right* problems. My dissertation explored how new technologies reshape professional boundaries and power structures. The question wasn't "can we automate this task?" but "what does this mean for human expertise, accountability, and trust?" Anthropic is one of the few companies that seems to be asking similar questions before deployment, not after.

I want to work here because I'm tired of watching companies optimize for quarterly growth at the expense of doing things correctly. I want to be the person who helps the world's most thoughtful organizations understand why your approach matters—and then gives them the roadmap to execute on it.

The work I want to do doesn't exist at most companies. It exists here.
