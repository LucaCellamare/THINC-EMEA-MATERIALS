# Why Anthropic?

I spent the last year in healthcare helping ophthalmologists decide whether to trust AI systems that could one day diagnose their patients' blindness. The conversations were never about features or performance benchmarks. They were about: *What happens when this gets it wrong? Who's accountable? How do I explain this to my patient?*

That's when I realized the companies that will matter aren't the ones racing to deploy first. They're the ones building systems people can actually trust when it matters. That's Anthropic.

Here's what I've learned about partner marketing: It's not about convincing skeptics through better slides. It's about finding organizations that already share your values but don't have the language or frameworks to act on them yet. My job at Topcon wasn't to "sell" healthcare systems on innovation—it was to help them articulate why it mattered to their patients and give them the tools to move forward with integrity. I drove 200% growth in engagement because I found the people who already cared and made it easier for them to act.

The partners Anthropic needs aren't the ones chasing hype. They're enterprise leaders and operators who understand that cutting corners now creates catastrophic risk later. The CMO who wants to transform customer service but won't sacrifice user privacy. The product team scaling globally who refuses to compromise on quality. The healthcare executive who sees the potential but can't afford to be wrong.

These are my people. I've spent two years coordinating cross-functional initiatives across five teams and two continents. I've worked with KOLs, startups, and health systems to build programs that balance innovation with responsibility. I speak seven languages because I learned early that meeting people in their context—their culture, their concerns—is the only way to build real partnerships. Not just transactional ones.

What excites me about Anthropic isn't just that you're solving hard technical problems. It's that you're solving the *right* problems. My dissertation explored how AI reshapes professional boundaries and power structures. The question wasn't "can we automate this task?" It was "what does this mean for human expertise, accountability, and trust?" Anthropic is one of the few companies asking these questions before deployment. Not after something breaks.

I want to work here because I'm tired of watching companies optimize for quarterly growth at the expense of doing things correctly. I want to be the person who helps the world's most thoughtful organizations understand why your approach matters and then gives them the roadmap to actually execute on it.

The work I want to do doesn't exist at most companies. It exists here.
